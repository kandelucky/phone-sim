Phone simulator: short guide

A phone on your computer screen, for trying out an app before it becomes a real Android app. No installation, no internet needed.

The simulator speaks other languages too: ⚙ → Language (on first start it follows your browser's language). The Info button (ⓘ) shows this same guide inside the simulator, in that language. Every button in detail: the full version.

1. Start

A phone on your computer screen: try an app here before it goes on a real Android phone. No install, no internet needed. This short version is for people who test the app; hold the mouse over a button for a short hint.

How to open it Double-click sim.html. It opens in your browser; Chrome or Edge work best. You see a phone with the app inside, and beside it or under it the console: the app's diary, what the app is doing and where it fails.

2. Buttons

All buttons are in the top bar. What each one does, in one line:

Open app Which app opens in the phone. Here you can also pick the app's folder or an .apk file, but only on the live demo or a local server (not when sim.html is opened by double-click). Under the field: the demo app and a few websites to try at once; Google, YouTube and most big sites do not open (Limits → Other websites). Download the demo app (zip).
Back The phone's back button: the previous screen, or closing a window. What it does depends on the app.
Background Like pressing Home. The app hides but does not close.
Return Brings the hidden app back. It should continue where you left it.
Close / Open Closes the app fully; the next Open starts it from scratch. Anything not saved is lost.
Rotate Turns the phone: upright ↔ sideways.
Mute Turns off all the app's sound.
Finger / mouse On the screen the mouse works like a finger or like a normal mouse. A finger is closer to a phone; the mouse is better for drop-down lists, sliders and selecting text.
Screenshot Saves a picture as a PNG file: the screen only, the whole phone or the whole window.
Video Records a video. Press, pick Screen or Phone, and in the browser's question pick this tab. Press again to stop; the file downloads by itself.
Presentation mode Hides everything but the phone. Good when you show the app to someone.
Reload Starts the app again. The console keeps its lines, so you can compare before and after.
Two phones A second phone next to the first: see the Two phones tab.
Device Which phone to show: Samsung, Pixel, iPhone, iPad and others. Numbers like 360 × 800 are the size the app sees.
100% Zoom How big the phone looks on your monitor. It does not change the app.
Console panel Where the console sits: on the side, below, or hidden.
Info This guide.
Settings The ⚙ panel: see the Settings tab.

3. Two phones

A second phone next to the first, with the same app. Good for seeing one app on two phones at once.

What for One app on two models at the same time: a small and a big phone, or upright and sideways.
Where The Two phones button, left of Rotate. The second phone opens in a small window over the first; press the button again to close it.
The window Drag its head to move it, any corner to change its size, ✕ closes it. Place and size are remembered.
Its own buttons Mute, Finger mode, Rotate, the device list and Zoom: each phone has its own.
Shared Open app and Reload work on both phones. Back, Background and Close work only on the first.
Console and screenshot Its lines in the console are marked ②. Screenshot → Whole window shows both phones.

4. Finger and zoom

Finger mode is on from the start: on the phone screen the mouse works like a finger.

Tap A white circle appears where the finger touches.
Drag Press and slide: a list or a page scrolls.
Pinch zoom The mouse wheel: up = zoom in, down = zoom out. Two circles show on the screen.
Mouse mode For what a finger cannot do: a drop-down list, a slider, selecting text.
Right click Opens the browser's menu; the app does not see it.
100% Natural size Zoom → Natural: the phone on the screen is as big as in your hand. Calibrate once: hold a bank card to the screen and match the outline. It is remembered.

5. Settings

⚙ opens the panel; every choice is remembered. The most useful ones:

Size Your own width × height, if your phone is not in the Device list.
Bars The top and bottom bars. Gestures = a thin line at the bottom, as on new phones. 3 buttons = ◁ ○ □. Hidden = no bars, as in a game.
Camera hole The front camera's hole in the screen. Keep it on to see whether it covers anything important.
Theme Colours: light, dark and more. The app changes its colours at once.
Volume 0 to 15, as on a phone.
Ads Ads come = a test ad shows. No ads = none comes: check that the app still works without ads.
Internet Off = as if the phone had no internet: check what the app does offline.
Font size Makes text bigger, as the phone's setting does. 130% and 200% show where the layout breaks.
Language The simulator's own language. The app inside the phone does not change.

6. Screen and console

Beside the phone's bottom right corner is a small ⓘ: click it to see what the app gets right now: the model, the size and how much of each edge a bar covers.

Console The app's diary: the app writes what it does. The simulator adds its own lines too: back, background, close, volume, vibration, link, ad.
Red line An error. Something broke, even if the screen looks fine.
Filter Small chips under the console head: App, Simulator, Touches, Warnings, Errors. Click = hide that kind, double click = only that kind.
Search Shows only the lines with this word.
⋮ More Copy or Save the lines you see now; Clear empties the console.
Data The tab beside the console: what the app saved on the phone. You can change a value or delete it.
abc Keyboard Tap a text field in the app: a keyboard comes up at the bottom. Check whether it covers a field or a button.

7. Checks

Five checks, about ten minutes. Every time, before you show the app to anyone.

1 Different sizes Small (Old Android 360×640), typical (Galaxy A15), big (iPhone 16 Pro Max) and, if the app is for tablets too, iPad. Nothing is cut off, no text runs out of its box, every button can be reached. If the app works both upright and sideways, rotate on each size.
2 Bars and camera With the camera hole on, switch the bars: Gestures, 3 buttons, Hidden. Nothing important (a button, a score, a title) hides under a bar or the hole.
3 Back Press it on every screen. It goes where the user expects. The app never gets stuck and never quits by surprise.
4 Background and close Use the app for a moment, then Background → Return: it continues where it was (timer, music, game). Then Close → Open: is the progress still there? What is lost here is lost on a real phone too.
5 Console After all of the above, there are no red lines.

8. Limits

The simulator is close to a phone, but it is not a phone. Always try the app on a real phone before you publish it.

Speed A computer is faster than most phones: what stutters a little here stutters more on a phone.
Vibration You see how long and in what rhythm it buzzes, not how strongly.
Other websites Google, YouTube, GitHub and most big sites forbid being shown inside another page: the phone shows “refused to connect”. That is the site's rule, not a simulator error. A site that does open only shows: finger mode and the console do not reach it. Your own app is not affected.
Touch is approximate One finger, or two fingers pinching in or out. No long-press, no fling, no free two-finger move.
Fonts A phone may use a different font, so text can come out a little wider or narrower.
Timers in the background In the background the simulator does not stop the app's timers and sounds. The app has to stop them itself.
Screenshot and video Chrome and Edge only.
APK Only the app's web part runs. Camera, files, notifications and other phone functions are not here, only stand-ins.
